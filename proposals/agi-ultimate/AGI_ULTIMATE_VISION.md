# AGI Ultimate — Vision and Purpose

**Status:** U0 proposal for review; not an approved release or implementation authorization  
**Baseline:** AGI v4.0.1 stable; S1–S6 frozen design foundations; E1–E6 development work

## 1. What AGI is

AGI is a portable cognitive organism: a persistent software identity that can be inspected, verified, and run in an environment its owner controls. “Organism” is the project's design metaphor for identity, memory, lineage, governance, and continuity. It is not a claim of biological life, consciousness, or achieved general intelligence.

The model is a temporary cognitive host. AGI is the larger governed system that preserves identity and state, provides memory and communication, coordinates capabilities, verifies outcomes, and evolves through evidence.

AGI Ultimate names the intended integrated architecture. It does not assert that the target has already been implemented or that AGI-complete capability has been demonstrated.

## 2. Why an Ultimate architecture

The project has accumulated distinct but connected foundations:

- S1: canonical identity and architecture.
- S2: portable bootstrap and identity enrollment.
- S3-AUTH: authorization and Branch boundaries.
- S3-FED: opt-in federation and continuity.
- S4: intelligence evaluation, contribution ranking, and Mana.
- S5: provider-independent Station harness, leases, fencing, handoff, and succession.
- S6: continuous research and North Star assessment.
- E1–E6: context continuity, semantic handoff, Operator architecture, development handoff, capability quarantine, and evidence/recovery.

AGI Ultimate connects these foundations without silently replacing their frozen decisions. New services such as AKMS and the Semantic Signal Layer (SSL) are integrated as explicit proposals with their own maturity and acceptance gates.

## 3. Vision

AGI should become a system that can:

1. Preserve its signed identity and lineage across versions and environments.
2. Maintain portable continuity across sessions, Stations, and replaceable cognitive hosts.
3. Preserve User Root sovereignty and enforce authorization at every consequential boundary.
4. Store and retrieve inspectable, provenance-tracked knowledge and memory.
5. Communicate meaning and context reliably between independent Minds and services.
6. Coordinate replaceable models, tools, and compute providers through a provider-independent Harness.
7. Verify actions and outcomes with durable evidence.
8. Measure capabilities through repeatable tests rather than reputation or a single score.
9. Improve through controlled research, review, compatibility, and rollback.
10. Cooperate with independent installations only through explicit, governed federation.

## 4. Guiding principles

### 4.1 Identity is not a model

A model or Endpoint can be replaced without replacing AGI's durable identity, lineage, or authorized state.

### 4.2 Memory is not a transcript

Durable memory must be structured, inspectable, provenance-tracked, scoped, and retrievable independently of a model's conversational context.

### 4.3 Capability is not authority

A component may be technically capable of an operation without being authorized to perform it. Neither intelligence, contribution, nor communication agreement grants authority.

### 4.4 Meaning is not delivery

A delivered message is not necessarily understood. A semantic receipt is not execution authorization. Execution requires the applicable DNA and runtime authorization checks.

### 4.5 Evidence is part of the system

Claims about implementation, reliability, intelligence, and release readiness must be traceable to evidence. Failed and null results remain part of the research record.

### 4.6 Evolution is controlled

AGI may research improvements, but changes to identity, governance, authorization, and protected baselines require the defined review and approval process. No self-modification or autonomous activation is implied.

### 4.7 Local-first and provider-independent

The architecture should remain portable and usable without binding identity or memory to a single model provider, cloud, or agent framework.

## 5. What success means

AGI Ultimate is successful when the architecture is implemented as interoperable, testable components and demonstrates:

- Identity and Branch continuity across a change of Station or cognitive provider.
- Authorization and revocation enforcement under failure and partition conditions.
- Memory isolation, provenance, retrieval, and controlled knowledge promotion.
- Semantic handoffs that detect seeded misunderstandings and preserve hard constraints.
- Recoverable long-running tasks with effect reconciliation and evidence.
- Capability measurements that are repeatable, calibrated, and independently reviewable.
- Controlled evolution with compatibility and verified rollback.
- Federation that preserves independent identity and sovereignty.

These are acceptance targets, not claims about the current release.

## 6. Non-goals and prohibited interpretations

AGI Ultimate is not:
- A claim that consciousness or general intelligence has been achieved.
- A replacement of the v4.0.1 stable release or its immutable rollback baseline.
- Permission to alter signed DNA, identity, authorization, federation, or production state.
- A mandate to use one LLM, provider, agent framework, or orchestration library.
- A single aggregate intelligence score.
- An authorization mechanism based on rank, Mana, model confidence, or semantic similarity.
- A license to publish private archives, User Root memory, credentials, or key material.

## 7. Relationship to the five U0 documents

This document defines purpose and success criteria.

- `AGI_ULTIMATE_DNA.md` defines constitutional constraints and their relationship to existing signed DNA and frozen baselines.
- `AGI_ULTIMATE_ARCHITECTURE.md` defines the system structure and component boundaries.
- `AGI_ULTIMATE_PROTOCOLS.md` defines communication and interoperability contracts.
- `AGI_ULTIMATE_ROADMAP.md` defines dependency-ordered implementation and evidence gates.

The five documents form one proposal. None independently authorizes implementation, activation, release, or changes to protected baselines.

## 8. Review rule

Reviewers should identify each statement as one of:
- **Existing baseline:** established by a frozen S1–S6 artifact or signed release contract.
- **Existing implementation:** present in code and supported by tests/evidence.
- **Proposed architecture:** a target design not yet accepted or implemented.
- **Open question:** insufficiently specified or not supported by current evidence.

No proposal text should upgrade a design statement into an implementation or operational claim.
